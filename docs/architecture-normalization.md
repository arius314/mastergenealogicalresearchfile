# Normalization

Perhaps not unexpectedly, this document covers aspects of the Master Genealogical Research File that require normalization. It is a work in progress; and constructive feedback/suggestions on normalization matters will be received with gratitude.

## Canonical IDs

IDs for every canonical entity (apart from meta) are created automatically at the time they're created.

They're composed of two parts. The first is a prefix that determines the type: `p_` for a `person`, `u_` for a `union`, `pc_` for a `parent_child`, `e_` for an `event`, `d_` for a `document`, `f_` for a `fan_entity`, and `n_` for a `note`. The second, concatenated to the first, is a unique hexadecimal number, not sequential but generated by `crypto.randomUUID()`. That means means $16^(32) \approx 3.4\times 10^{38}$ possible entities of each type - more than enough for any genealogy.

The sheer number of possibilities of random numbers chosen by `randomUUIID()` makes the probability of a collision small - but it's still nonzero. Thus upon entity creation, the proposed generated ID will (once the `randomUUID()` code is created) first be checked to see if it appears at all among existing IDS; if it does not, it gets accepted and assigned to the new entity, but if it does appear, then a new `randomUUID()` will be generated and tested.

As the code (to be created) will remove links to an entity being deleted, the ID of a deleted entity will be available for re-use by another entity if (however unlikely) it happens to be randomly selected.

## Deduplication

Deduplication involves identifying and merging duplicate entities with each other.

This is necessary, to keep the family tree and its data clean and accurate. However, it also cannot be automated. I've seen enough cases on Ancestry and in other places, where an AI searching for potential duplicates suggested merging two people as duplicates when they clearly weren't. (The most forgivable cases were those where a family recycled a deceased child's name, giving it to another child; but I've also seen first and second cousins with the same names confused for each other, and - what I consider pretty bone-headed - brothers with different names being treated as duplicates!) I also find automatic detection annoying when it triggers for anyone I haven't yet found a birthdate for (there's at least one common app out there I can't stand for this reason). Overall, AI and other machinery can be helpful in finding duplicates, but the merge must be confirmed or rejected by a human.

Right now two mechanisms are in the roadmap for deduplication matters:
- the ability for the user to select two potential duplicates, review their information, and merge them; and
- the ability to convert a FAN into a `person` who's a relative, or merge that `FAN` with a `person`, should that FAN turn out to be a relative after all. (This is only partially a deduplication matter, and will be the only case where an entity of one type may be merged with an entity of another.)

Beyond this, deduplication-related matters remain fuzzy and undecided in my mind.

## Naming Normalization

The normalized name of a `person` entity is that person's birth name. This, the person's `display_name`, is a string without structure.
- If the person's birth name is unknown but their married name is, one may express the `display_name` as something like "Sarah (unknown)". The "(unknown)" can be changed when the birth surname is found.
- The adoptive name of an adoptee or foundling may be used instead, especially if the birth name is unknown.

This may not be the only name someone uses in life - we have to deal wtih preferred names, nicknames, Anglicized immigrants' names, legally changed names, aliases, adoptive names, dit names, married names, religious names, and titles, possibly others.
- The `names` field of a `person` is a list; and each alternate name is a separate entry in the `names` list.
- Entries in the `names` list are structured, in the form {"type": "X", "title_prefix": "", "given": "", "surname": "", "title_suffix": ""}. The `type` and at least one other field in the entry is required; however, for these entries, surnames and Christian names are kept separate.

## Date Normalization

The date normalization scheme used here appears pretty standard - I don't see a need to deviate far from what's already common on Ancestry and other apps on this front.

Acceptable date syntax - for use in any normalized date `event.date.normalized` or `event.date.second_normalized` for this app - is as follows, for display and (possibly in future, though that's undecided) calculations:
- The standard date format recognized by this app is DD MMM YYYY.
- "BC" can appear after the year, if it's relevant.
- Also acceptable are YYYY and MMM YYYY. (If they're to be used in calculations: Assume 01 JAN YYYY and 01 MMM YYYY respectively if `data.time_modifier` is "approximate", "floruit", "before" or the first `normalized` date for "between", but 31 DEC YYYY and (final day of month) MMM YYYY for "after" or the `second_normalized` date for "between".)
- Double years (for instance 31 Jan 1749/1750) are not allowed in normalized dates. They may appear in the `date.original` field which doesn't require normalization. But in normalized dates:
    - if there is no `date.calendar` entry, or if that entry is "Gregorian", the higher year is required;
    - but if `date.calendar = "Julian"`, the lower year is required (as the Julian year is assumed to begin on 25 March).

Every date has a `time_modifier` entry, which governs both the label used in display and (as seen above) calculations.
- Allowed values are "exact", "approximate", "before", "after", "between" and "floruit".
- If `time_modifier = "exact"`, there is no display label. For "approximate", "before", "after" and "floruit", the label "ABT", "BEF", "AFT", "BET" and "FL" (respectively) appears in capital letters before the date. For "between" - the only case with a second date - the display will be "BET (date 1) AND (date 2)".

At present only the Gregorian and Julian calendar formats are allowed (and the Julian calendar is marked when used). I'm undecided on whether support for alternate calendars (eg. Jewish, Hijri, Byzantine, French Revolutionary, English regnal) will be eventually provided, or if any machinery will be provided in due course to convert dates between Gregorian and alternate calendars.

## Place Normalization

Whether there will be place normalization, or what it would look like, remains undecided. I have an idea of how large any database of normalized place names would be, especially when the villages so many of our ancestors lived in were included and when abandoned settlements are taken into account - and of the time and gazeteer resources needed to create such a database; and these seem prohibitive to me.

What is more firm, is that if place normalization occurs, it should be relative to the time an event occurred or a document was created. (Thus, for instance, Perinton in western NY state should be treated as part of Monroe county from Monroe's creation in 1821 onward, but part of Ontario County in 1820.) This, in the interest of ensuring people look at the right jurisdiction to find documentary evidence.
